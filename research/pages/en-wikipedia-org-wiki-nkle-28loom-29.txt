**Band weaving** is the hand production of narrow woven fabric. This fabric may be called tape, band, [inkle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inkle_(loom) "Inkle (loom)"), strap, belt, back strap, trim, and more.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-:1-1):10 It can be accomplished on a variety of types of looms, including inkle, band, tape, [backstrap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loom "Loom"), and [rigid heddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heddle "Heddle") looms.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-:0-2):171–172 Hole and slot heddles are also designed to weave bands.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-3):11 Depending on which loom is used, the material could be [warp-faced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_and_weft "Warp and weft") or a balanced weave.

### Inkle

Inkle weaving is a type of warp-faced [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving "Weaving") where the [shed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shed_(weaving) "Shed (weaving)") is created by manually raising or lowering the [warp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warp_(weaving) "Warp (weaving)") yarns, some of which are held in place by fixed [heddles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heddle "Heddle"). According to the _Oxford English Dictionary_, the term _inkle_ has several meanings, the first of which is "A kind of linen tape, formerly much used for various purposes." The derivation of the word is uncertain.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-4) Inkle weaving is commonly used for narrow work such as trims, straps and belts. Inkle weaving is done on a loom known as an inkle loom. One key element that differentiates inkle looms from other band looms is that a continuous warp is required.[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-5):1

#### History

A table-top inkle loom was patented by Mr. Gilmore of Stockton, California in the 1930s but inkle looms and weaving predate this by centuries.

Inkle weaving was referred to 3 times in [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare "Shakespeare"): in _[Love's Labour's Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love's_Labour's_Lost "Love's Labour's Lost") (Act III, Scene I),_ [Pericles, Prince of Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericles,_Prince_of_Tyre "Pericles, Prince of Tyre") (Act V), and in [The Winter's Tale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Winter's_Tale "The Winter's Tale") (Act IV, Scene IV).[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-6) Jonathan Swift mentioned inkle weavers in his _[Polite Conversations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polite_Conversation "Polite Conversation")_.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band_weaving#cite_note-:0-2):172
